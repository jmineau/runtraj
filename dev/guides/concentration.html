Concentration Grids#

Use this page when you want what HYSPLIT is best known for: how much of a pollutant is where, on a grid, over time. HYSPLIT counts its particles on the grids you give it and writes a concentration file for each. runtraj runs it in that mode with mode: concentration in the hysplit block, and gives you each release’s grids as one xarray.DataTree, a xarray.Dataset per grid. We recommend it when you need concentrations or deposition and not the path of each particle: the result is far smaller than the particle table.

One run#

A concentration run needs at least one pollutant, what is released, and at least one grid, where it is counted. Here a pollutant TEST is released at 1 unit of mass per hour for one hour from Salt Lake City, and counted for 3 hours on a grid of 0.02° cells around the release, in a layer 100 m deep, as hourly averages:

In [1]: import runtraj

In [2]: from runtraj.models import run_model

In [3]: from runtraj.models.hysplit import HysplitConfig, MetConfig

In [4]: start = runtraj.PointRelease(
   ...:     time="2021-07-15 01:00", longitude=-111.85, latitude=40.77, altitude=10
   ...: )
   ...: 

In [5]: config = HysplitConfig(
   ...:     n_hours=3,
   ...:     numpar=1000,
   ...:     mode="concentration",
   ...:     pollutants=[{"name": "TEST", "rate": 1.0, "hours": 1.0}],
   ...:     grids=[{"name": "slc", "spacing_lat": 0.02, "spacing_lon": 0.02, "levels": [100]}],
   ...:     krand=2,
   ...:     seed=42,
   ...: )
   ...: 

In [6]: tree = run_model("hysplit", start, config, MetConfig.model_validate(met_dir)).result

In [7]: slc = tree["slc"].to_dataset()

Each pollutant is a variable of its own name (never lat, lon, z or time, which are coordinates). Its unit is the unit of mass of its rate per cubic meter, so with a rate in grams per hour, the concentration is in g/m³. Pollutants and grids#

pollutants and grids are lists, one record each. Every pollutant is released at every release location and counted on every grid. A pollutant is HYSPLIT’s four-letter identifier and its emission, and, for a pollutant that is removed, its deposition, wet removal and decay in HYSPLIT’s units (Pollutant). All zero, the default, is a gas that is not removed.

A grid is a regular latitude-longitude grid (ConcentrationGrid): its center (unset, the release; we recommend this for releases at many places), its spacing and span in degrees, its levels, and its samples. sampling is average (the mean over each interval, the default), snapshot (the value at the interval’s end), or maximum; sampling_interval is the length of a sample in minutes: any number under an hour, and from an hour up a whole number of hours (60, 120, …), since HYSPLIT reads the interval in whole hours and a setting such as 90 is refused. A level of 0 counts deposition on the ground. Every grid samples from the start of the run to its end.

In config.yaml:

models:
  hysplit:
    n_hours: 24
    numpar: 5000
variants:
  particles: {}
  conc:
    mode: concentration
    pollutants:
      - {name: SO2, rate: 1000.0, hours: 24, deposition_velocity: 0.006}
    grids:
      - {name: local, spacing_lat: 0.01, spacing_lon: 0.01, span_lat: 1, span_lon: 1, levels: [0, 100]}
      - {name: regional, spacing_lat: 0.1, spacing_lon: 0.1, span_lat: 10, span_lon: 10, levels: [500, 3000], sampling_interval: 360}

The particles variant makes particles as before, and conc makes concentration from the same releases. They never share a settings folder.

What a concentration run does not have#

The particle file’s settings (varsiwant, outdt, veght) and emisshrs belong to the particles mode. A concentration run leaves them out, so a hysplit block that sets them for its particle variants serves a concentration variant too; each pollutant has its own hours. A pollutants, grids or conversions without mode: concentration is refused. A stage reads particles unless its class says another type (its result), so a stage of particles is refused on a concentration variant; give the variant <stage>: null under stages. The particle transforms and the plots of particles are for particles too. runtraj.run_trajectories() runs one release in any mode, and returns the tree of grids in this one.

runtraj does not yet expose time-varying emissions (EMITIMES) or sampling that starts or stops inside the run; open an issue if you need one.

Other things HYSPLIT can count#

HYSPLIT’s ICHEM switches a concentration run to count something else. It is the setting ichem, 0 unless you set it, and it is part of the run’s settings. We recommend leaving it out until you need one of these:

ichem

What you get

What it needs

1

A grid for each point of the release: which source each cell’s pollutant came from. The pollutant’s variable gets a source dimension.

One pollutant; a multipoint release (a column release is refused, since it is one source).

10

A grid for each hour, or each qcycle hours, of emission: when each cell’s pollutant was released. The variable gets a release_time dimension.

One pollutant; a forward run; qcycle in whole hours, which n_hours is a multiple of; hours of the pollutant no longer than qcycle.

2

Pollutants that convert into one another at a rate per hour, such as SO2 into sulfate.

Two pollutants or more, maxdim set to their number, and conversions.

6

Mass per mass of air in place of mass per volume: the concentration divided by the air density at the particle.

Nothing.

9

A lowest level that reaches from the ground to a share of the mixed layer, wherever each particle is.

The first of each grid’s levels as a percentage, 1 to 100, and no level of 0.

5

Deposition of whole particles by chance, in place of a share of every particle’s mass.

maxdim: 1 and initd: 0, which are the defaults, and a pollutant that deposits.

With ichem: 10 one run tells apart the pollutant of each hour, where you would otherwise add a release per hour to releases.csv and run each:

variants:
  by_hour:
    mode: concentration
    ichem: 10
    qcycle: 1
    n_hours: 24
    pollutants:
      - {name: CH4, rate: 1.0, hours: 1}
    grids:
      - {name: local, spacing_lat: 0.01, spacing_lon: 0.01, levels: [100]}

ch4 = sim.concentration["local"]["CH4"]  # (release_time, time, z, lat, lon)
ch4.sel(release_time="2021-07-15 03:30")  # what was released from 03:30
ch4.sum("release_time")                   # all of it, as ichem 0 counts

Each cycle is released at rate for hours, so with a rate of 1 the grids are what one unit of mass per hour in that cycle gives, and you scale each by the emission you want to test. With ichem: 1 the same holds for each point of the release, and source numbers the points from 1 in their order, with source_lon, source_lat and source_height beside it.

Conversions are a list, as pollutants are, each naming two of them:

conc:
  mode: concentration
  ichem: 2
  maxdim: 2
  pollutants:
    - {name: SO2, rate: 1000.0, hours: 24}
    - {name: SO4, rate: 0.0, hours: 24}
  conversions:
    - {source: SO2, product: SO4, rate: 0.01, weight_ratio: 1.5}

rate is the fraction converted per hour, and weight_ratio the mass of the product per mass converted (1.5 for SO2 to SO4).

The other values are refused, each with its reason (The concentration mode). ichem: 8 is HYSPLIT’s particle output, which is mode: particles: there ichem may be 8 or left out, and a trajectory run reads none.

In a project#

A project runs a concentration variant as it runs any other. Each release’s result is one NetCDF file, a group per grid, under concentration/ in the output directory (Output Layout). Read them back by simulation:

project.status()  # a concentration column, beside particles
results = project.concentration()  # {(release, variant, realization): DataTree}
sim = project.simulation(release_id, "conc")
sim.concentration["local"]["SO2"].sel(z=100).sum("time")

The grids of two releases are centered on each release, so they are not joined into one array: each simulation keeps its own tree. A grid with a center of its own is the same for every release, and xarray.concat() over results joins them along a new dimension. Result Types and Concentration describes the result and its file.
